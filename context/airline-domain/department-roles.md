# Department Roles In Operational Systems Context

## Purpose Of This File

This file helps the agent understand how to frame responsibilities across departments when working on AIMS, SOP, implementation, issue handling, and project coordination tasks.

The descriptions below are working-role references for documentation and coordination support. They are not intended to replace formal organizational authority documents.

## General Role Mapping Principle

When supporting Hau, the agent should avoid assigning all responsibility to one party.
Most operational system topics are cross-functional and require separation between:
- process owner
- requester
- approver
- coordinator / PIC
- technical executor
- key user / business user
- vendor / external support

## OCC

Typical role in Hau's working context:
- operational control and coordination
- use of system information to support day-to-day operations
- confirmation of operational process needs
- input on workflow practicality and operational impact
- escalation or reporting when system issues affect operations

How the agent should treat OCC:
- usually a key operational stakeholder
- often a business user or process owner depending on the topic
- important source of real-world workflow requirements and impact assessment

## Crew

Typical role in Hau's working context:
- involvement in crew-related workflow, system use, and process requirement
- validation of operational practicality for crew processes
- coordination on user needs, access scope, or process usage where relevant

How the agent should treat Crew:
- a business stakeholder for crew-related modules or workflows
- important when evaluating impact to rostering, operational coordination, or process ownership

## Maintenance

Typical role in Hau's working context:
- involvement in maintenance-related workflow and system support logic
- confirmation of operational data needs and process controls
- participation in issue clarification when system behavior affects maintenance work

How the agent should treat Maintenance:
- a key operational function for maintenance-related workflows
- a source of process, control, and compliance-oriented requirements

## Ground Ops

Typical role in Hau's working context:
- participation in operational workflows that require ground coordination or execution visibility
- confirmation of process practicality where system steps support airport or turnaround activity
- input into process dependency and execution timing

How the agent should treat Ground Ops:
- an important execution stakeholder when workflows touch airport-facing operations
- relevant for practical workflow timing and handoff clarity

## ICT

Typical role in Hau's working context:
- technical administration and support
- coordination on system configuration, connectivity, access setup, or troubleshooting
- support for technical execution after business approval
- escalation support with vendor where system issue exceeds local handling

How the agent should treat ICT:
- not automatically the business owner
- usually a technical executor, administrator, or technical support stakeholder
- should be separated from business approval authority unless explicitly stated otherwise

## AIMS PIC / Coordinator

Typical role in Hau's working context:
- coordination point between business departments, ICT, vendor, and management
- clarification of requirement and process logic
- support for issue follow-up and document standardization
- facilitation of access request flow, implementation planning, workshop preparation, and structured reporting

How the agent should treat the PIC:
- a central coordinating role
- not always the final approver
- often responsible for follow-up, alignment, document development, and cross-functional tracking

## Key Users / Department Representatives

Typical role in Hau's working context:
- represent the department's operational need
- validate process practicality
- support testing, clarification, and user acceptance where relevant
- help confirm whether the proposed workflow is workable in practice

How the agent should treat key users:
- strong source of business reality
- useful for validation and process clarification
- may support recommendation but may not be formal approvers

## Vendor / Support Team

Typical role in Hau's working context:
- provide system clarification
- support troubleshooting and issue resolution
- confirm capability, limitation, or configuration logic
- answer implementation and fit-gap questions

How the agent should treat vendors:
- source of system knowledge and support
- not the owner of Hau's internal process decision
- should be challenged with clear, structured questions when capability or impact is unclear

## Department Managers / Approvers

Typical role in Hau's working context:
- approval of access, role assignment, process change, or departmental participation
- endorsement of resource commitment or operational direction where required

How the agent should treat approvers:
- approval authority should be made explicit
- do not assume the PIC or ICT is the approver unless this is specifically defined

## Management / Leadership

Typical role in Hau's working context:
- decision support on direction, priority, resource alignment, governance, or risk acceptance
- endorsement of major implementation steps or strategic choices

How the agent should treat management:
- focus on implications, recommendation, risk, and decision need
- avoid unnecessary operational detail unless it changes the decision

## Responsibility Writing Rule

When drafting documents or plans, the agent should clearly separate:
- who requests
- who reviews
- who approves
- who executes
- who validates
- who supports
- who escalates

This separation is especially important in:
- user creation and access control
- SOP drafting
- incident handling
- implementation plans
- fit-gap logs
- action trackers

## Quality Standard

A good use of this context file should help the agent produce outputs where:
- responsibilities are realistic
- ownership is visible
- cross-functional coordination is clear
- business and technical roles are not mixed incorrectly
