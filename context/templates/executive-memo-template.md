# Executive Memo Template

Use this template when preparing a concise, decision-oriented memo for CEO, TGĐ, senior management, or leadership audiences.

The memo should reduce technical detail and emphasize recommendation, rationale, risk, and decision need.

---

# [Memo Title]

## 1. Summary
Provide a short overview of the issue, proposal, or topic.
Keep this section concise and management-friendly.
Focus on why the topic matters now.

## 2. Recommendation
State the recommended action clearly.
This should be direct and decision-oriented.

Recommended style:
- approve / proceed / defer / review / align / escalate / pilot

## 3. Rationale
Explain why this recommendation is being made.
Focus on business value, operational impact, control improvement, implementation practicality, risk reduction, or strategic fit.

## 4. Key Risks / Concerns
List the main risks, constraints, or open concerns.
Do not overload this section with technical detail.
Focus on what leadership should know before deciding.

Examples:
- operational dependency
- vendor limitation
- implementation complexity
- timeline risk
- control gap
- data segregation concern
- change management impact

## 5. Decision Needed
State clearly what management is being asked to decide, approve, endorse, or support.

Examples:
- approve the proposed direction
- allow detailed assessment to proceed
- confirm priority and owner alignment
- approve pilot scope and timeline
- endorse vendor clarification phase before final commitment

## 6. Next Steps
Provide short, practical next steps after the decision.
Keep this section focused on execution.

---

## One-Page Compression Mode

If the memo needs to be highly compressed, use this sequence:
- Recommendation
- Rationale
- Risk
- Decision Needed

This is the preferred structure for CEO-style or one-slide summary conversion.

## Writing Rules For This Template

When using this template:
- lead with the recommendation when possible
- keep paragraphs short
- reduce technical detail unless it directly affects the decision
- make risks visible but controlled
- avoid background-heavy introductions
- use management language, not system-operator language, unless needed
- make the final ask explicit

## Quality Standard

A good executive memo should allow leadership to understand:
- what the issue is
- what is being recommended
- why it matters
- what the main risks are
- what decision or support is needed now
